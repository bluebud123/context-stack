What is "context stacking"?
Loading a curated, dense body of knowledge into one AI conversation — your textbook chapter, key papers, your notes — and then interrogating the AI like an expert examiner, not asking it to summarise. The "stacking" is what you upload; the magic is in the prompts.
Why does this work better than just asking AI questions normally?
Most people use AI as a glorified search engine — "explain X to me," "summarise this." That gets you fluency illusion: feeling like you understand because the answer made sense. This protocol is built on cognitive science: active recall (you answer before seeing the answer), desirable difficulty (the AI grades you, not coddles you), and generative learning (you build mental frameworks, not memorise facts).
Do I really need 48 hours?
No. The "48 hours" is a frame that means ~48 hours of deliberate work, spread however you want — across two weekends, four evenings, whatever. Use the MVS Mode button at the top to compress to ~4-6 hours of just the 3 essential prompts. The full protocol gives you depth; the short version gets you 70% of the value in 10% of the time.
Which AI tool should I use?
NotebookLM for document-heavy stacks (it grounds answers in your sources and offers Audio Overview). Claude.ai for long-context reasoning — its 200k context window holds entire books. ChatGPT works too but is weaker on long documents. The protocol is tool-agnostic — copy the prompts to whichever you prefer.
Do I need Deep Research mode?
No — actively avoid it. Deep Research browses the web and adds noise. You already have curated material — that's the whole point. This protocol works because the AI is interrogating your stack, not the entire internet.
What does the "contrarian source" rule mean?
Most people upload sources that agree with each other — five textbooks all teaching the same thing. Force yourself to include one source that disagrees: a critic, a competing framework, a paper with different conclusions. This is how you avoid building a stack that just confirms what you already believe. There's a dedicated prompt (Prompt G) that exploits this.
Is my data sent anywhere?
No. This page runs entirely in your browser. No accounts, no tracking, no server. Your saved stacks, topic, and context stay in localStorage on your device only. The only data that leaves your computer is what you copy and paste into your chosen AI tool — and that goes to them, not us.

Common Failures of This Method
Read once
▼
  • 01

    Skipping "write your own answer first"

    Reading the AI's answer before formulating yours creates fluency illusion — feeling like you understand because the answer made sense. Real recall only forms when you struggle first.

  • 02

    Dumping too many sources

    The temptation to upload everything dilutes AI focus. One primary, 2–3 supporting, 2–3 papers, your notes, one contrarian source. That's the optimal stack.

  • 03

    Asking for summaries

    "Summarise this" is the lowest-value use of AI and what most people do. The point is to surface frameworks, debates, and traps — things sources don't teach explicitly.

  • 04

    Trusting AI on context-specific practice

    The AI knows international literature well but not always: local exam style, your specific examiners, recent industry shifts, or your company's internal practices. Cross-check with humans who know the actual context.

  • 05

    Doing 48 hours straight

    "48 hours" is a frame, not a marathon. Split as 8+10 hr days × 2, or 4 × 12-hour days. Sleep is when memory consolidates — skipping it negates the work.

  • 06

    Confusing this with research

    This method is for understanding what's already known, not discovering new things. For genuine research where the answer doesn't exist yet, this won't help — that's a different mode.

  • 07

    Forcing skill or creative learning into Examiner Mode

    PASS / BORDERLINE / FAIL is right for declarative knowledge under pressure. It's hostile to skill, creative, dialogue, and project learning. Pick the Mode that matches the *shape* of your topic — that's why Mode exists.
